Arbitrage and hold

Hold is how far two implied probabilities sit from 100%. Most pairs at a single book sit above 100%, and that gap is the book's margin. Arbitrage is when the two prices add up to less than 100%. Then a split stake, one ticket on each price, locks in a profit either way. This arbitrage calculator doesn't place either bet.

How to use it

  1. Book A and Book B take American odds. Both start at -110.
  2. Total to split ($) starts at 100. That's your combined stake, not your profit.
  3. Hold, Combined implied, Fair A, and Fair B always show. If the prices arb, the page adds Locked profit, Return, Stake A, and Stake B, and the hint says: These prices arb. The split locks the same profit either way.

A blank or bad price gets you: Enter two American prices and a stake to split. If there's no arb, the hint quotes the combined implied percent and says an arb needs that number under 100%. No stake split shows.

The formula in plain terms

Decimal odds are your return per $1, stake included. -110 is 1.90909. -105 is 1.95238. +120 is 2.20.

inv = 1/decimalA + 1/decimalB. That's the same as adding the two implied probabilities. Combined implied is inv as a percent. Hold = (inv minus 1) times 100.

If inv is 1 or more, there's no arb. Fair A and Fair B are the no-vig American prices.

If inv is under 1, hold shows negative. Stake A = total times (1/decimalA) / inv. Stake B = total times (1/decimalB) / inv. That sizes both outcomes to the same return. Locked profit = that return minus the total. Return = locked profit / total.

Hold and return aren't the same number with the sign flipped. Hold is (inv minus 1). Return is (1/inv minus 1). At inv = 0.9667, hold is -3.33% and return is 3.44%.

Worked example 1. Defaults

Book A -110, Book B -110, total 100.

Each side implies 52.38%. Combined implied = 104.76%. Hold = 4.76%. Fair prices are -100 and -100. inv is above 1, so there's no arb.

You'll see those four rows and the hint: No arb. Combined implied is 104.76%. An arb needs that under 100%. No stakes show. Forcing a $50 and $50 split would lose about $4.55, which is why the split stays hidden.

Worked example 2

Set Book A to -105, Book B to +120, and the total to 250.

inv = 1/1.95238 + 1/2.20 = 0.9667. Combined implied = 96.67%. Hold = -3.33%. Fair prices round to -113 and +113.

Stake A = $132.45 on -105. Stake B = $117.55 on +120. Together that's $250. More money goes on -105 because that side pays less per dollar.

Either side returns about $258.60. Locked profit is +$8.60. Return is 3.44%, which is $8.60 / $250, not the absolute value of the -3.33% hold.

You'll see Hold -3.33%, Combined implied 96.67%, Fair A -113, Fair B +113, Locked profit +$8.60, Return 3.44%, Stake A $132.45, Stake B $117.55, plus the arb hint.

When it helps, and where it stops

Use it to read the margin, and to size both sides only when combined implied is under 100%. A positive hold means those two prices don't lock a profit.

The numbers assume both bets fill at the prices you typed, with no commission and no line move while you're clicking. Sportsbooks may limit accounts or void bets. The split is for the two opposite sides of one two-way result. It doesn't cover a draw, a parlay, or two different games. FAQ

Why do -110 and -110 say no arb?

Each side implies 52.38%. Together that's 104.76%, and the hold is 4.76%. An arb needs combined implied under 100%. The fair pair is -100 / -100, and the page doesn't show a stake split.

Why is return 3.44% when hold says -3.33%?

They measure different things. On -105 / +120, combined implied is 96.67%, so hold is 96.67 minus 100 = -3.33%. Return is your locked profit divided by your stake: $8.60 / $250 = 3.44%. A negative hold means the prices overlap. Return is how much of your split stake that overlap pays.

Why is the larger stake on the minus price?

The split evens out the payout, not the stake. -105 pays less profit per dollar than +120, so more dollars have to go on -105. In the $250 example, that's $132.45 on -105 and $117.55 on +120.

Does EdgeTilt place the two bets?

No. The page only does the math. You still have to get both bets filled at those prices. Nothing gets sent to a sportsbook or saved to your account.

What if both prices are from the same book?

Then you're measuring that book's hold, same as the no-vig page, and it'll almost never arb. A real arb is usually the best price on one side at one book against the best price on the other side somewhere else.

Can the total to split be any amount?

Yes. Stakes and locked profit scale with the total, but the return percent doesn't. $250 at -105 / +120 locks +$8.60, which is 3.44%. Double the total and the dollar profit doubles. The 3.44% stays put.
