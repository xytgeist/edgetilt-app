Closing line value

Closing line value is your number against the close, not against the opener. The close is the last number before the event starts. Beat the close and you got a better number than the market's final word, even if the ticket loses.

This page works out the gap from two figures you type. It doesn't look up a game, a close, or your bet log. Positive means you beat the close. Spreads and totals come back in points, and prices come back in probability points. Those units don't mix.

How to use it

  1. Market starts on Spread. Enter Your line and Closing line, same side. Both numbers are for the side you bet. The form starts at 7 and 3.
  2. Total: pick Over or Under. Under starts selected. Your total and Closing total start at 45 and 48.
  3. Price: Price you bet and Closing price take American odds, starting at +150 and +130.
  4. Read Line CLV or Price CLV, plus the sentence above the number.

If a figure's missing, you'll see "Enter your number and the close." or "Enter the price you bet and the closing price." The page won't make up the close for you.

The formula in plain terms

Spread, same side: your line minus the closing line. A dog at +7 that closes +3 is 7 minus 3 = +4. A favorite at -3 that closes -6.5 is -3 minus -6.5 = +3.5. Enter both numbers for the side you bet. Don't put your +7 against the other team's minus line. That sign flip isn't a close, and the math will be wrong.

Over: your total minus the close. Over 47.5 closing at 46 is +1.5. Under: the close minus your total. Under 45 closing at 48 is +3.

Price CLV = (implied close minus implied bet) times 100. Plus price implied = 100 / (price + 100). Minus price implied = absolute price / (absolute price + 100). If the close is shorter than your price, that's positive.

Line CLV shows one decimal with pts after it. Price CLV shows two decimals. Above zero: You beat the close. Below zero: The close beat your number. Zero: Same number as the close. That is not a beat.

On a price result, the page adds: Price CLV is probability points, not the spread points stored on a bet. The bet tracker stores spread and total points on the bets you logged. It doesn't put this price figure in that column.

Worked example 1. The spread already filled in

Your line 7, closing line 3. 7 minus 3 = 4. You'll see "You beat the close." and Line CLV +4.0 pts. That's closing line value, not the final margin, and it doesn't mean the bet won.

Worked example 2. Price, then the total

Switch to Price and leave +150 and +130. +150 implies 40.00%, and +130 implies 43.48%. The gap is +3.48 pts. You'll see "You beat the close. Price CLV is probability points, not the spread points stored on a bet." and Price CLV +3.48 pts.

Switch to Total. Leave Under, 45, and 48. Close minus your total is 48 minus 45 = +3. You'll see "You beat the close." and Line CLV +3.0 pts.

Flip Side to Over with the same 45 and 48. Your total minus the close is -3. You'll see "The close beat your number." and Line CLV -3.0 pts. Same totals, other side, opposite sign.

When it helps, and where it stops

Use it when you've got both numbers in front of you. Positive CLV means you beat the last number. It doesn't promise the ticket cashes.

The page won't fetch the close for you. If you only have the other side of a spread, convert it to your side before you type it. Price points and line points don't add together. Nothing you type gets sent to EdgeTilt.

FAQ

Does a positive CLV mean the bet won?

No. It means your number beat the close. The game can still go the other way. Closing line value grades the price you got. It doesn't settle the ticket.

What if I only wrote down the other team's spread?

Convert it to your side first. If you bet the dog at +7, both boxes should be plus numbers for that dog. Putting +7 against the favorite's closing -3 counts the sign twice. Your line 7 and closing line 3 is the +4.0 example already in the form.

Why is price CLV +3.48 when the odds only moved from +150 to +130?

The move is 20 cents in American odds, but this row measures probability points. +150 implies 40.00% and +130 implies 43.48%. The difference is 3.48 points of probability. The page won't call that 20 points, and the bet tracker doesn't store that 3.48 as spread CLV.

Does this look up the closing line for me?

No. You type your number and the close. The page doesn't read a scoreboard, a market file, or your account.

What does 0.0 pts mean?

Your number and the close are the same. The verdict says: Same number as the close. That is not a beat. Set a spread to -3 and -3 and Line CLV is 0.0 pts.

Is the total side important?

Yes. Under 45 closing at 48 is +3.0 pts. Over 45 closing at 48 is -3.0 pts. Pick the side you bet before you trust the sign.
