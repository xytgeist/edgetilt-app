Kelly criterion

The Kelly criterion tells you how much to stake. If your win percent is right, full Kelly is the slice of bankroll with the highest expected growth at that American price. It doesn't pick a side for you. You bring the price, your percent, and whether you want the full fraction, half, or a quarter.

The math runs in your browser. It doesn't place a bet, read a sportsbook, or store anything you type. Half is selected by default, because full Kelly swings hard when your percent is even a little off.

How to use it

  1. Bankroll ($): the money you've set aside for betting. The form starts at 1000.
  2. American odds: type +150 or -110, and keep the plus sign on a plus price.
  3. Your win percent: your own number, from just above 0 to just under 100. Not the percent baked into the odds.
  4. Kelly fraction: Full, Half, or Quarter.
  5. Read Stake, Bankroll percent, Full Kelly, and Fraction used (100%, 50%, or 25%).

Type a bad price and you'll see: Enter American odds such as +150 or -110. Leave out the bankroll or the percent and it asks for a bankroll and a win percent between 0 and 100. If your percent is too low for the price, you get no stake, and the hint says: No edge at that win percent. Kelly says sit this one out.

Kelly fraction

The formula in plain terms

b is your profit per $1 staked, not the return with your stake added back. On a plus price, b = price / 100, so +150 means b = 1.50. On a minus price, b = 100 / the absolute price, so -110 means b = 0.90909.

p is your win percent as a decimal, and q is 1 minus p. Full Kelly = (b times p, minus q) / b. If that comes out zero or negative, the stake is zero. Half multiplies a positive fraction by 0.5, and Quarter multiplies it by 0.25. Stake = bankroll times that reduced fraction.

The hint also shows the book's implied percent, but Kelly doesn't treat that as your edge. Plus price: 100 / (price + 100). Minus price: absolute price / (absolute price + 100). +150 implies 40.0%, and -110 implies 52.4%.

Worked example 1. Defaults already in the form

Bankroll 1000, odds +150, win percent 45, Half.

Full Kelly = (1.50 times 0.45, minus 0.55) / 1.50 = 0.125 / 1.50 = 8.33%. Half of the exact fraction is 4.17%. Stake = $41.67.

You'll see Stake $41.67, Bankroll percent 4.17%, Full Kelly 8.33%, Fraction used 50%. The hint says the book implies 40.0%, and that Kelly is using your 45%, not that number.

Worked example 2

Set bankroll to 5000, odds to -110, win percent to 55, and fraction to Quarter.

b times 0.55 is 0.50. Take away 0.45 and you're left with 0.05. Full Kelly = 0.05 / 0.90909 = 5.50%. A quarter of that shows as Bankroll percent 1.38%. Stake = $68.75.

You'll see Stake $68.75, Bankroll percent 1.38%, Full Kelly 5.50%, Fraction used 25%. The book implies 52.4%.

Now drop win percent to 30 on that same -110, at any fraction. The fraction goes negative, so the page shows no stake and tells you to sit this one out.

When it helps, and where it stops

Use it once you already trust your win percent and want a ceiling on the stake. It can't fix a bad percent. It also only sees one bet at a time, so it doesn't know about your other tickets or what a book will actually take. It's American odds only. FAQ

Why is the implied percent different from the percent I typed?

The implied percent is the break-even rate baked into the price. +150 implies 40.0% because 100 / (150 + 100) = 40. Kelly uses the win percent you typed. You only get a positive stake when your percent actually beats the price on the formula, not when the two just look close.

What does "sit this one out" mean?

Your win percent is too low for that American price, so the full Kelly fraction isn't positive. The page shows no stake. Bumping the fraction from quarter to full won't create an edge that isn't there.

Should I use full Kelly?

Full Kelly is the fraction the formula gives you, and it assumes your win percent is exact. If your percent is noisy, full Kelly overbets. That's why Half is the default here. Quarter is the smaller step. The page will still work out full Kelly if you pick Full.

Can I type decimal or fractional odds?

Not on this form. It's American odds only, like +150 or -110. Convert 2.50 or 3/2 on the odds converter, then paste the American result here.

Does this place a bet or save it to my tracker?

No. The math runs in your browser and nothing gets sent to EdgeTilt. Sports Bet Tracker is a separate account tool, and it doesn't read this form.

Why did half of 8.33% become 4.17%, not 4.16%?

The 8.33% on the page is 0.08333... rounded to two decimals. Half of the exact fraction is 0.041666..., which rounds to 4.17%. The stake uses the exact fraction too, so $1,000 times that half is $41.67, not $41.60.
