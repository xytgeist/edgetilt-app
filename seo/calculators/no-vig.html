No-vig fair odds

A two-way market has two sides and no draw: a spread, a total, or a moneyline in a sport that can't tie. The book prices both sides so the implied probabilities add up to more than 100%. That extra is the hold, also called the juice or the vig. Fair odds are the same two sides with the hold divided out, so the two true chances add up to 100% and neither price carries the book's margin.

This no-vig calculator does that for American odds, right in your browser. It doesn't look up a line, compare two books, or save your prices. Comparing two books is a job for the arbitrage calculator. This one handles a single market.

How to use it

  1. Enter Side A in American odds. The form starts at -110.
  2. Enter Side B in American odds. The form starts at -110.
  3. Read the rows. Hold is the margin as a percent. Overround is the two implied probabilities added together, also as a percent. Fair A and Fair B are the no-vig American prices. True A and True B are the no-vig win percents.

If either box is empty or isn't a real American price, the only line you'll see is: Enter both sides in American odds, such as -110 and -110.

The formula in plain terms

First, turn each American price into an implied probability.

Plus price: implied = 100 / (price + 100). +130 implies 100 / 230 = 43.48%.

Minus price: implied = absolute price / (absolute price + 100). -150 implies 150 / 250 = 60.00%. -110 implies 110 / 210 = 52.38%.

Add the two implied probabilities together. That sum is the overround. Hold = (overround minus 1) times 100. A normal market at a book lands a few points over 100%, so the hold comes out positive.

Fair probability for a side = that side's implied probability divided by the overround. The two fair probabilities add to 100%. The page rounds each one to American odds. At 50%, that's -100. Above 50% it's negative, and below 50% it's positive. Rounding is why 57.98% shows as -138 instead of a long decimal price.

Worked example 1. The numbers already in the form

Side A -110, Side B -110.

Each side implies 110 / 210 = 52.38095%.

Overround = 52.38095% + 52.38095% = 104.7619%, which shows as 104.76%.

Hold = 4.7619%, which shows as 4.76%.

Fair probability on each side = 52.38095 / 104.7619 = 50.00%.

Fair American odds at 50% are -100 and -100.

You'll see Hold 4.76%, Overround 104.76%, Fair A -100, Fair B -100, True A 50.00%, True B 50.00%. That 4.76% is the book's margin. It's not a lean toward either side.

Worked example 2. An uneven market

Change Side A to -150 and Side B to +130.

Side A implies 150 / 250 = 60.00%. Side B implies 100 / 230 = 43.47826%.

Overround = 103.47826%, which shows as 103.48%. Hold = 3.47826%, which shows as 3.48%.

True A = 60.00 / 103.47826 = 57.983%, which shows as 57.98%.

True B = 43.47826 / 103.47826 = 42.017%, which shows as 42.02%.

Those two true percents add to 100%. The fair American prices round to -138 and +138.

You'll see Hold 3.48%, Overround 103.48%, Fair A -138, Fair B +138, True A 57.98%, True B 42.02%.

When it helps, and where it stops

Use it when you've got both sides of one market and want the no-juice price. It doesn't ask for your win percent. That's what Kelly is for.

It only handles two-way markets. Leave the draw out of a three-way moneyline and the hold will be wrong. Both prices should be the two sides of the same market at the same book. The best price at one book against the best price at another is an arbitrage question, not a hold question. Fair odds are a benchmark. A book doesn't owe you -100 just because -110 / -110 devigs to a coin flip. Nothing you type gets sent to EdgeTilt.

FAQ

Why are fair odds different from the price I was offered?

The price you're offered has the hold baked in. Fair odds divide each side's implied probability by the total, so the margin's gone. -110 / -110 is a 4.76% hold, and the fair pair is -100 / -100.

What is the difference between hold and overround?

Overround is the two implied probabilities added up. Hold is how far that sum sits above 100%. On -110 / -110, the overround is 104.76% and the hold is 4.76%. They're two ways of describing the same margin.

Why did -150 / +130 become -138 / +138?

-150 implies 60.00% and +130 implies 43.48%. The sum is 103.48%. Divide each side by that sum and you get 57.98% and 42.02%. Rounded to American odds, those percents are -138 and +138.

Does this work with a draw on the board?

No. The formula needs exactly two sides that cover every result. If you leave the draw out, the hold won't be the real three-way margin.

Will a book pay me the fair price?

Not on its own. Fair odds are the no-vig number, a benchmark. The price you're offered is whatever the book posted.

Does this use my EdgeTilt account or my bets?

No. Both prices stay in your browser. The bet tracker doesn't fill in this form, and this form doesn't read the tracker.
